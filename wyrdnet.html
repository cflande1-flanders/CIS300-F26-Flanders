What if 15,000 years doesn't mean 15,000 years everywhere?

One of the biggest timeline questions in the Maasverse comes from Lidia Cervos.

Lidia reveals that she is descended from Brannon, tying her bloodline directly to Throne of Glass and Erilea. At the same time, Midgard's history places the great migration and the arrival of the Asteri roughly 15,000 years in the past.

At first glance, those timelines can seem difficult to reconcile. But that assumes something the books have never actually established:

A year may not be the same length on every world.

MoreLess

This theory does not require time to move faster or slower on Midgard or Erilea. The same amount of physical time could pass on both worlds. Hours, days, and other measurements of elapsed time could function normally while the length of a year differs.

A year is determined by a world's own astronomical cycle. If Midgard and Erilea have different yearly cycles, the same span of physical time could produce a different number of calendar years on each world.

In other words:

15,000 Midgard years do not necessarily equal 15,000 Erilean years.

Why it matters

Midgard's history tells us that roughly 15,000 Midgard years have passed. But without knowing the length of an Erilean year, we cannot assume that Erilea would record that same span of time as 15,000 years.

That difference could reconcile Lidia's established ancestry with Midgard's 15,000-year history without changing either piece of canon.

More broadly, it means that any timeline comparison between Maasverse worlds has to account for a simple unknown: we do not yet know whether their years are the same length.

What to watch for

Future books could confirm or weaken this theory with surprisingly small details: differences in calendars, season lengths, day lengths, astronomical cycles, or any direct comparison of dates between worlds.
